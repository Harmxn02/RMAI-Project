\subsection{Guardrail Effectiveness}

% = PARAGRAPH 1 = Measurement caveat upfront

The error counts reported here are derived from the validator's output, which is a proxy for semantic correctness rather than a direct measure of it. As previously mentioned, while the validator provides a useful automated assessment of HTML validity, it may not capture all aspects of semantic correctness. We report error and warning counts as our primary metrics, but the results should be interpreted with this caveat in mind.

\subsubsection{Overall before/after results (feedback condition)}

% = PARAGRAPH 2 = Overall before/after results (feedback condition)

Overall, across all models and prompts in the feedback condition ($n=553$ runs), we observed a significant reduction in both errors and warnings after applying the guardrail. The aggregate results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:rq1-aggregate}. The mean error count decreased from 17.16 to 4.07, representing a 76.3\% reduction, while the mean warning count decreased from 6.00 to 1.49, representing a 75.2\% reduction. Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirmed that these reductions were statistically significant ($p < 0.001$), with 95\% confidence intervals indicating meaningful decreases in both metrics.
